# Privacy Policy SunMoment (English)

*Last updated: 13 November 2026*

SunMoment is an app for households, with or without solar panels. It tells you when your appliances are best run: on your own solar power or in the cheap hours of your contract. Short answer: **the app uses almost no data, and we cannot read any of it unless you send it to us yourself.** If you link your household, a small script of ours keeps your shared log, encrypted. We cannot read it.

## What stays on your phone
Your language and country, chosen town, panels, roof direction and tilt, appliances and timer settings, your answers to "Was yesterday's forecast right?", the daily yields you enter, your "Started" ticks (unless you link your household, see below), and the last downloaded forecast. In off-grid mode, your battery, your daily use and the battery level you enter also stay on your phone. There is **no account**; the app asks no name, email, address or phone number, uses no GPS, and contains no ads and no analytics or tracking software. On Android, cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer are **switched off** for this app. On iPhone and iPad, iOS includes the app's data in your own backup (iCloud or your computer) if you have backup switched on. You and Apple manage that backup; we have no access to it. New phone? Under Settings → Move to a new phone you make a file with your settings and your log yourself; it also contains your town. Your household's key and your purchase are not in it. The app does not send that file anywhere: you choose where it goes. It is also a readable copy of your data. Or set the app up again, which takes a minute.

Also on your phone: how you pay for electricity (your contract, your prices and cheap hours, and in Denmark your grid company), your runs in the cheap hours and the tips you tick, and, for the trial and the purchase card, on which days the app could help you and what you paid.

Also on your phone: in the Netherlands, your feed-in costs per kWh if you enter them; the shade on your roof; and for an electric car, how much you usually charge and by what time it must be full. Under Privacy & sources is the list **What did the app do online?**: the app's last 50 internet requests, with the time, the service, what was sent in broad terms and whether it worked. If the app cannot read a setting or a log when it starts, it keeps the old text once, so nothing is lost. That list and that copy go nowhere, not even in a feedback message. With **Share as a picture** you make a picture of your month: your runs and kWh, without your name or town, and on Android with a link to Google Play. You choose who you share it with.

Also only on your phone, even if you link your household: your week schedule, a period when you are away and your quiet hours. If you link your household, your house's settings do go along, encrypted, such as your town, roof, appliances, contract and prices; see the table below. With **Add to calendar** you put a run in your calendar: on Android your calendar app opens with the event already filled in, otherwise the app makes a calendar file (.ics). With **Log as a spreadsheet (CSV)** you make a file with your runs. Just like the move file, the app does not send these files anywhere: you choose where they go. The app does not read your calendar. Also only on your phone: how full your home battery is now (if you enter it), how many kWh your home battery gave a run, and which appliances you skip today with "Not today". If you have more than one house (**Homes**), the app keeps a full copy of the settings and logs of each other house, also only on your phone. Only the house you are using now goes into the file for a new phone. The household link belongs to one house: while you use another house, syncing waits until you use that house again.

**Camera** (only with "Scan the code"): the app uses the camera to read a household member's pairing code. This happens on your phone; no photo is stored or sent.

**Linking your meter or inverter** (only if you do it yourself, under Settings → Link your meter or inverter): the app looks on your own wifi for a HomeWizard P1 meter and then reads the device you link (a HomeWizard P1 meter, an inverter from Fronius, KOSTAL, SMA, SolarEdge or another brand with SunSpec, or a Victron or Zendure home battery) directly, only while the app is open. That traffic stays in your home network: the app only sends requests to addresses in your own network and refuses other addresses. Nothing goes to us or to the maker. The device's address is in your settings on your phone (also in the file for a new phone), but does not go to your household. The app does not keep what the device measures. On iPhone and iPad, iOS first asks whether the app may use your local network. A HomeWizard meter with the new API (v2) gives the app a key after you press the meter's button. That key is kept only in your phone's secure storage (not in the file for a new phone); the app first checks the meter's certificate, and unlinking deletes the key. Some devices only speak plain http, without encryption. Android cannot allow that per address, so it is allowed for the whole app; the app itself sends such requests only to addresses in your own network.

It works only if you link your household, and it keeps only encrypted blocks that we cannot read. If you give your phone a name for your household (for example "Anna"), that name travels inside those encrypted blocks; only your household sees it. As a brake on misuse, the script counts how many new links come from one internet address per hour. Apart from that, the app sends us nothing by itself.

## Your rights
You can ask for access to, correction, deletion, restriction or transfer of the data we hold about you (only messages you sent us). Contact: gala.apps11@gmail.com.

**Right to object:** you can always object to processing based on legitimate interest. If you do not want your IP address to reach Bright Sky or Bunny.net, the app cannot fetch a forecast; uninstalling the app stops it at once. No longer want to share your log with your household? Then unlink.

You do not have to give us anything. Without the weather request, the app just cannot show a forecast. The app makes no decisions about you and creates no profile.

Delete everything on your phone yourself: uninstall the app, or choose Settings → Apps → SunMoment → Storage → Clear data (Android). On iPhone and iPad, delete the app; an old iCloud backup is deleted in your iPhone's or iPad's settings. You can complain to the data protection authority in your country: Netherlands – Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; Belgium – Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorité de protection des données; Germany – your state's authority or the BfDI; Austria – Datenschutzbehörde; Switzerland – EDÖB (the Swiss data protection act also applies to you; your rights are similar); Liechtenstein – Datenschutzstelle; Luxembourg – CNPD; United Kingdom – ICO (the UK GDPR applies to you); France – CNIL; Denmark – Datatilsynet; other countries – the data protection authority of your country, if there is one. Your own country's privacy law may also apply; we give everyone the rights above, wherever you live.

## Purchases
After the trial (on Android at least 21 and at most 60 days, on iPhone and iPad 21 days) you can buy SunMoment once. On Android this runs entirely through Google Play, on iPhone and iPad entirely through Apple's App Store; Google's or Apple's terms and privacy policy apply. Google Play's purchase function in the app may send technical data about the purchase to Google; Google's privacy policy applies to that. We receive no payment details. Google only shows us order details, such as the order number, the country and the amount; Apple only shows us sales figures, such as the number of sales per country and the amount, without anything that identifies you (such as your name or Apple Account). We keep those details for 7 years, as Dutch tax law requires (legal basis: legal obligation, Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR). At every start, the app asks the store whether you bought SunMoment. On Android it also asks Google Play whether buying is possible on this phone. If it clearly is not (for example in a country without paid apps), the app stays free. The answers stay on your phone. On iPhone and iPad, the trial starts with a free "purchase" in the App Store ("21-day Trial"). The app keeps only the date the App Store gives for it, on your phone, to count the 21 days; Apple sends us nothing about it that identifies you. So when your trial started is also stored only on your phone.

## Links to sources
If you tap a source in the app (for example under the tips), your browser opens that website. The website then sees your IP address, as with any visit. This only happens when you tap.

## Children
SunMoment is not made for children and does not ask anyone's age.
